# Homeowner starter guide to domestic retrofit (UK)

This note summarises official and government-endorsed starting points for householders who want to improve the energy efficiency of an existing home. It is general guidance, not a property-specific design or survey.

## What “retrofit” means

Retrofit means upgrading an existing home so it uses less energy, stays warmer (and healthier), and can move away from fossil-fuel heating where appropriate. Typical measures include insulation, draught-proofing, ventilation improvements, better windows and doors, heating controls, heat pumps, and solar generation.

## A practical order of work

1. **Understand your home** — Check whether you have an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) and what it recommends. For England and Wales, use GOV.UK “Find ways to save energy in your home” for tailored improvement ideas, estimated costs and savings, and next steps. You can also speak to an adviser on 0800 098 7950.
2. **Maintenance and easy wins first** — Fix damp, leaks, broken seals, and basic draughts before major works. Low-cost steps (LED lighting, heating controls, loft top-ups where suitable) often come first.
3. **Fabric first** — Improve the building envelope (loft/roof, walls, floors, windows/doors, airtightness) so the home retains heat.
4. **Ventilate right** — Tighter homes need planned ventilation. Poor ventilation after insulation can increase condensation and mould risk (“build tight, ventilate right”).
5. **Low-carbon heat** — Consider heat pumps or other clean heat once the fabric is improved or as part of a coordinated plan. Check heat-pump suitability guidance on GOV.UK.
6. **Generation and storage** — Solar PV and batteries can follow once demand is reduced.
7. **Use competent people** — Prefer TrustMark-registered businesses for energy-efficiency work and MCS-certified installers for renewables and heat pumps. For deeper multi-measure projects, a retrofit assessment and whole-house plan (PAS 2035 pathway) reduces the risk of conflicting measures.

## Funding and schemes (England — check current eligibility)

National routes change over time; always verify on GOV.UK. Common pathways include:

- **Help to Heat / energy grants hub** — Overview of Boiler Upgrade Scheme, Warm Homes: Local Grant, Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund, Energy Company Obligation (ECO), and related support.
- **Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS)** — Upfront grants toward eligible heat pumps (and limited other technologies) in England and Wales; installer-led applications; Ofgem publishes property-owner guidance.
- **Warm Homes: Local Grant** — Local-authority delivered upgrades for eligible low-income private households (owner-occupiers and some private renters).
- **Energy Company Obligation (ECO)** — Supplier-led energy-efficiency measures for eligible households; contact obligated suppliers; local authorities may refer under ECO Flex.
- **Warm Home Discount and bill support** — Separate from physical retrofit works but relevant for fuel-cost pressure.

Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland have distinct advice lines and programmes (for example Home Energy Scotland; Nest in Wales; NI programmes).

## Quality and consumer protection

- Choose installers yourself where possible; be wary of cold-call sales.
- Get multiple written quotes; do not pay large deposits up front.
- Government-endorsed quality: **TrustMark** (home improvements / energy efficiency) and **MCS** (microgeneration).
- Citizens Advice publishes guidance on finding installers and spotting scams.

## London and local support

Support is often delivered with local partners. Examples (availability and eligibility vary):

- Greater London Authority **Warmer Homes** / Warm Homes: Local Grant consortium delivery for eligible Londoners.
- Borough schemes such as free home energy advice visits (for example Green Doctors partnerships in some boroughs).
- Local Citizens Advice energy advice lines.

Always check your own council’s energy and cost-of-living pages.

This starter note is for orientation only. Measure suitability depends on construction type, condition, heritage status, tenure, and household needs. Take professional advice before major works.
